Roof Leak Water Damage in Livermore: What to Know

Where you see the stain and where the rain gets in are usually two different places. A loose shingle, a failed flashing joint or an aging seal around a skylight lets rain in, and it travels across the roof deck and framing until it falls into the insulation. A ceiling stain tends to arrive late, well after the space overhead first got wet. Our team maps everything that got wet, then dries it.

We are a water damage crew, not a roofing company. Our job is the inside of the house: pulling out soaked insulation, drying the framing and drywall, treating for mold and putting the ceiling back together. If rain is still coming in, we can tarp the roof as a temporary cover, or work alongside the roofer you choose so the leak is fixed before we close anything up.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut Off Wet CircuitsTurn off the breaker for any light, fan or outlet that has water running through or near it, then keep your hands off it.
  2. Contain the WaterPut a pan or bucket beneath each drip, and shift rugs, electronics and furniture clear of the wet area. Lay towels or plastic sheeting down to protect the floor.
  3. Relieve a Ceiling BulgeA ceiling that sags with trapped water can be drained: stay off to the side and make a small opening at its lowest spot, with a bucket underneath.
  4. Stay Off the RoofClimbing on slick shingles or crawling around a dark attic is not worth the risk. Note from inside where the water is coming in and leave the rest to professionals.
  5. Document What You SeeBefore anything is moved or discarded, get video and photos of the attic, the ceiling and damaged items, and then phone us so drying can begin.
Will Insurance Pay?

What About Insurance?

Homeowners policies often cover interior water damage when a roof leak is sudden and accidental, such as wind tearing off shingles or a limb puncturing the roof. Leaks blamed on age, wear or skipped maintenance are usually denied, and the roof repair itself may be handled differently from the interior. Photos and moisture readings from us go to the adjuster, though only your policy decides coverage, and your agent can explain it.

Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • Gaps at chimneys, vents and sidewalls where the flashing has separated or rusted through.
  • Roof valleys where worn metal or piled debris lets water get under the covering.
  • A fallen limb or large hail that punctures or bruises the roof covering.
  • Rubber boots around plumbing vent pipes that split after years in the sun.
  • Water backing up at the eave and under the shingles because the gutters are blocked.

What the Water Damages

Ceiling Drywall

Drywall that stays wet stains, sags and loses its strength. The tape releases along cracked seams, and with enough weight a section can come down suddenly.

Ceiling Fixtures and Wiring

Leak water heads for openings in the ceiling, such as recessed lights, fan boxes and junction boxes. Keep that circuit off until someone has inspected it, since damp wiring may corrode or short out.

Attic Insulation

Once soaked, insulation flattens, loses its value and keeps the drywall under it damp for days. In some cases fiberglass batts dry out and stay. Removal is the usual answer for wet cellulose.

Inside the Walls

From the attic, a leak can drop into a stud bay and soak the wall insulation and drywall from behind. You might notice nothing more than a peeling patch or a puffy baseboard.
