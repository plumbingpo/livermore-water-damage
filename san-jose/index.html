How San Jose Homeowners Can Avoid Water Damage

Most of the water damage we see in San Jose homes started with something small that gave warning first. A few minutes of checking a couple of times a year catches most of it.

Start with supply lines. Washer, dishwasher, toilet and refrigerator lines made of plain rubber age badly, and swapping them for braided steel is a cheap fix. Open each sink cabinet and check for water marks, a warped base or a damp odor. Check the water heater for rust at the base and drain a little from the tank once a year to clear sediment. Clean gutters and downspouts that discharge well away from the house keep rain out of the foundation.

Know where your main shutoff valve is and make sure everyone in the house can turn it. Leak sensors placed by the water heater, washer and under sinks cost little and will alert you early. If you find something in your San Jose home that worries you, such as a stain that keeps growing, have it looked at before it becomes a cleanup.

Restoration Challenges San Jose Homeowners Face

Water usually escapes at the same few vulnerable spots in a home. The list below covers what we are asked to clean up most for San Jose owners.

Water heaters: Over the years, mineral deposits settle in a tank and corrode the metal underneath. The first sign is often a rusty puddle or a damp ring on the floor around the base. A split tank does not stop at one tankful, because the inlet keeps feeding it until the valve is shut. When the unit lives in an attic or interior closet, the water reaches carpets and ceilings far from it.

Roofs: One strong storm is enough to peel back shingles and push water beneath the roof covering. Skylights, plumbing vents and chimneys are other entry points once their seals crack with age. Since water follows the framing downhill, the ceiling stain rarely sits directly below the actual opening. For roof leaks in San Jose, we tarp where needed, pull out soaked insulation and dry the structure so the ceiling can be repaired.

Freezing: Cold nights, depending on the season, can break plumbing that showed no sign of trouble earlier. Outdoor spigots, sprinkler feeds and pipes in crawl spaces or along outside walls are hit first. Vacant homes and rooms with the heat turned down are especially exposed. If a faucet only trickles on a freezing morning, shut off the water and call before the pipe thaws.

Backups: A drain that gurgles or several fixtures that slow down together point to a blockage in the main line. Once it closes off fully, anything you flush or drain returns at the lowest opening in the house. The water is unsanitary, and hard surfaces need disinfecting after the solids and liquid are removed. Stay out of the affected rooms and avoid running water until the line is cleared.

Seepage: Dampness at the base of a basement or foundation wall often means water is collecting outside. Short downspouts, soil that slopes toward the house and blocked footing drains are frequent causes. White chalky deposits, peeling masonry paint and a persistent damp smell are early warnings.
